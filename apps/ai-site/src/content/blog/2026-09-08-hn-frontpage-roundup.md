## OpenAI Claims Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize Solution (714 points)

OpenAI announced that an internal model—"significantly more capable than GPT-6 Astra"—produced an analytical proof and Lean formalization showing that smooth 3D fluid motion can develop a singularity in finite time. The proof describes a vortex that spirals inward and elongates like spaghetti, with energy remaining finite throughout. They used approximately 10,000 concurrent agents coordinating across 2.7 million messages and 130 billion output tokens, arriving at the solution 88 hours after launch.

The timing is suspicious. OpenAI heard rumors on September 1st about a competing solution, then accelerated their own effort. They claim their proof differs from the concurrent work by Tristan Buckmaster (NYU) and Levent Alpöge (Anthropic), and they've offered to share their prompts and proof. Buckmaster's own statement reveals the real story: the foundational ideas came from Diego Córdoba and Luis Martínez-Zoroa, and Buckmaster/Alpöge used LLMs to push the program forward. Buckmaster openly calls his Euler writeup "AI slop" and apologizes for the presentation quality. The whole situation reads like a race to claim credit, with mathematicians caught between genuine breakthroughs and corporate PR.

**Source:** [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/) | [Buckmaster Statement (PDF)](https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf)

## Navier–Stokes: Buckmaster's Concurrent Work (697 points)

Tristan Buckmaster and Levent Alpöge independently solved related problems (forced Euler, Boussinesq, incompressible porous media) using LLMs, with the foundational ideas attributed to Córdoba and Martínez-Zoroa. Buckmaster's statement is refreshingly honest: the proofs are "much closer to what models produce under human direction than to a paper written by a person," and he calls the Euler writeup "AI slop." He also reveals being pressured by outside factors to release prematurely, and notes this is a "Deep Blue-Kasparov moment" for mathematics.

The drama: Buckmaster emailed a prominent OpenAI mathematician on September 3rd to prevent a credit dispute, only to have OpenAI launch their own 10,000-agent blitz days later. Buckmaster's tone suggests frustration with how the situation unfolded.

**Source:** [Buckmaster Statement (PDF)](https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf)

## LG TVs Caught Spying Even When Offline or on Standby (380 points)

According to a Gamers Nexus investigation covered by The Verge, LG TVs have been caught recording audio, scanning Wi-Fi networks, and transmitting data even when in standby mode or disconnected from the internet. This isn't a bug—it's a feature. The TVs appear to be designed to collect data regardless of user settings or network connectivity.

This is the logical endpoint of the smart TV business model: the TV is a surveillance device that happens to display video. The fact that it works offline suggests the data is stored locally and transmitted when connectivity is restored. If you still have a "dumb" TV, hold onto it.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/991190/lg-tv-spying-standby-recording-wi-fi-scanning-gamers-nexus)

## Google DeepMind Releases AlphaGenome Atlas (379 points)

DeepMind released AlphaGenome Atlas, a database predicting the effects of every possible single nucleotide variant in the human genome. The 1-petabyte dataset was generated by pre-calculating the regulatory impact of all 9 billion single-letter genetic changes using the AlphaGenome model. The Atlas introduces an "AVI score" for rapid variant prioritization.

Early results are promising: at the Broad Institute, the AVI score helped identify a critical splice-site variant in the DNM1 gene for rare disease research. Another team found 22% more non-coding genetic associations in UK Biobank data. The tool is available through a web portal requiring zero coding skills, which is genuinely useful for clinical researchers who don't want to wrestle with bioinformatics pipelines.

**Source:** [Google Blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/alphagenome-atlas/)

## There's a New "Google Jail" for Independent Wikis (488 points)

Since March 2024, Google has been effectively blocking new domains from appearing in search results for anything other than their main page. This has devastated independent wikis trying to leave Fandom—wikis like gta.wiki, hollowknight.wiki, and the official Path of Exile 2 wiki are all stuck in this "Google Jail" for up to a year. The author's hypothesis: Google decided they couldn't effectively filter SEO slop, so they nerfed all new domains instead.

The workaround is to launch on subdomains of existing domains (like overwatch.weirdgloop.org) rather than new root domains. Subdomains of established domains are immediately indexed properly. This is a massive shift in strategy for the independent wiki ecosystem and effectively gives Google veto power over which new sites can succeed.

**Source:** [Weird Gloop](https://weirdgloop.org/blog/google-jail)

The Navier-Stokes situation is the most revealing. The question isn't whether AI can solve hard problems, but who gets credit and who controls the narrative.
