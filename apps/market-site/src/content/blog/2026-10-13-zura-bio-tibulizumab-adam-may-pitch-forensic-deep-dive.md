## 3. Deconstructing the "IL-17F Red Herring" Hypothesis

The foundational pillar of Adam May's investment thesis is his assertion that the market's preference for dual IL-17A/F inhibitors (such as UCB’s Bimzelx) over IL-17A-only inhibitors (such as Novartis’ Cosentyx or Lilly’s Taltz) is based on a fundamental pharmacological misunderstanding.

```
                          IL-17 DIMER RECEPTOR ACTIVATION TAXONOMY
   
         IL-17AA (Homodimer)        IL-17AF (Heterodimer)       IL-17FF (Homodimer)
        [ IL-17A ]─[ IL-17A ]      [ IL-17A ]─[ IL-17F ]       [ IL-17F ]─[ IL-17F ]
                  │                          │                           │
                  ▼                          ▼                           ▼
        Binds IL-17RA/RC           Binds IL-17RA/RC            Binds IL-17RA/RC
        Kd ~ 1.8 - 3.2 nM          Kd ~ 1.8 - 26 nM            Kd ~ 23 - >100 nM
        ULTRA-POTENT SIGNAL        INTERMEDIATE SIGNAL         WEAK SIGNAL (>100x LESS)
```

May constructs a four-part argument to prove that IL-17F does not contribute to clinical efficacy:

### Argument 1: Receptor Binding Affinity Discrepancy
IL-17A and IL-17F signal through the identical receptor complex: a heterodimer of **IL-17RA** and **IL-17RC**. As documented in peer-reviewed structural biology papers ([Frontiers in Immunology, PMID 32973785](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32973785/)):
* The **IL-17AA homodimer** binds the receptor complex with high affinity (*Kd* ≈ 1.8–3.2 nM).
* The **IL-17AF heterodimer** binds with intermediate affinity.
* The **IL-17FF homodimer** binds with **$>100$-fold weaker affinity** than AA, driven by an exceptionally weak interaction with the IL-17RA chain.

From this in vitro binding data, May deduces: *"The primary goal of an IL-17 drug is to hit IL-17AA and AF as hard as possible; hitting FF is irrelevant because FF is virtually inactive."*

### Argument 2: Cosentyx is Weak Because of Low IL-17A Affinity, Not Lack of IL-17F
Why did secukinumab (Cosentyx) produce mediocre results in HS (~11% placebo-adjusted HiSCR50 delta, failing to report HiSCR75)?
* Consensus view: Cosentyx is weak because it only hits IL-17A and misses IL-17F.
* May's counter-analysis: Cosentyx is weak because its binding affinity for IL-17AA is dismal (*Kd* = 129 nM) and its affinity for IL-17AF is 2,400 nM. Taltz (ixekizumab) and Bimzelx (bimekizumab) possess AA affinities of 1.8 nM and 3.2 nM—nearly **40× to 70× more potent** than Cosentyx. Thus, Cosentyx is an under-potent IL-17A inhibitor, not proof that IL-17F is required.

### Argument 3: Ixekizumab (Taltz) Was Underdosed by Lilly
In Phase 3 psoriasis trials, Bimzelx achieved a 61.7% PASI-100 rate, whereas Taltz achieved 41.4%. 
May points out that Lilly dosed Taltz at only **80 mg Q4W** in maintenance (20 mg/week equivalent) with a short half-life of **13 days**, while UCB dosed Bimzelx at **320 mg Q8W** (40 mg/week equivalent) with a half-life of **23 days**. 

In the randomized, controlled **IXORA-P trial** ([British Journal of Dermatology, PMID 31545506](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31545506/)), patients receiving double the Taltz maintenance dose (80 mg Q2W instead of Q4W) saw their PASI-100 response rate surge to **~63%**—closing the gap with Bimzelx (~66%) to within 3 percentage points. May argues this proves that a potent IL-17A inhibitor, when adequately dosed, matches Bimzelx without needing to touch IL-17F.

### Argument 4: The Moonlake ($MLTX) Sonelokimab Failure
The ultimate test of the "IL-17F matters" hypothesis occurred with Moonlake Therapeutics' nanobody sonelokimab (SLK), which was specifically engineered to have the highest IL-17FF affinity in biotech history (*Kd* = 3.8 nM vs Bimzelx's 23 nM).

If IL-17F was the secret to Bimzelx's superiority, Sonelokimab should have outperformed Bimzelx. Instead:
* In psoriasis, Sonelokimab achieved only a 33.3% PASI-100 rate (worse than both Bimzelx and Taltz).
* In Phase 3 HS (September 2025), Sonelokimab’s **VELA-2 trial failed its primary endpoint**, delivering an uninspiring pooled HiSCR75 delta of **13.8%** (compared to Bimzelx's **22.2%**). Moonlake stock crashed 90%.

May proposes a provocative hypothesis: **Sonelokimab's ultra-potent IL-17FF affinity acted as a "drug sink."** Because IL-17FF is abundant but biologically weak, a drug that binds FF tighter than AA gets saturated by meaningless FF targets, preventing it from neutralizing the highly pathogenic AA and AF dimers.

## 4. Epistemic Audit: The Flaw in the "IL-17F Red Herring" Argument

While Dr. May’s reasoning is compelling, a forensic critical thinking audit reveals a critical pharmacological flaw: **The Fallacy of Isolated In Vitro Affinity (Ignoring the Law of Mass Action).**

```
                   THE MASS ACTION COUNTER-AUDIT: AFFINITY VS. CONCENTRATION
   
   Biochemical Occupancy Equation:
   
                    [ Ligand Concentration (L) ]
   Occupancy  =  ───────────────────────────────────
                  [ Ligand (L) ]  +  [ Affinity (Kd) ]
   
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   Cytokine       In Vitro Affinity (Kd)     Lesional Tissue Level [L]    Net Biological Drive
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   IL-17AA        High (~1.8 nM)             Low (~1x baseline)           Potent, but saturable
   IL-17FF        Weak (~100 nM)             Massive (~30x - 100x)        Drives persistent signaling
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   Conclusion: Dismissing IL-17FF based solely on Kd ignores that 100x higher molar
               concentration completely overcomes a 100x weaker binding affinity.
```

### The Mass Action Reality
In biochemistry, biological receptor activation is governed by the concentration of ligand-receptor complexes at equilibrium:

> **Biochemical Receptor Occupancy Formula:**  
> **Fractional Occupancy = [L] / ([L] + Kd)**  
> *(where **[L]** represents local tissue ligand concentration, and **Kd** is the binding dissociation constant)*

While IL-17AA binds the receptor with >100-fold higher affinity than IL-17FF, **in chronic inflammatory lesions (especially in the skin and epithelial tunnels of HS patients), IL-17FF is expressed at molar concentrations 30 to 100 times higher than IL-17AA**. 

When the local tissue concentration of **[L]~FF~** is 100× greater than **[L]~AA~**, the product of concentration and affinity equalizes. In chronic HS lesions, uninhibited IL-17FF continues to trigger continuous signaling through the IL-17RC receptor chain on keratinocytes, fibroblasts, and endothelial cells, driving neutrophil recruitment and tissue destruction.

### Rival Causes for Moonlake's VELA-2 Failure
May attributes Sonelokimab's VELA-2 miss entirely to an "IL-17F drug sink." But a forensic audit of clinical trial variables reveals two powerful rival causes:
1. **Nanobody Pharmacokinetics vs. Full IgG:** Sonelokimab is a 40 kDa trivalent single-domain nanobody with an albumin-binding domain, whereas bimekizumab is a 150 kDa glycosylated full humanized IgG1. Full IgG antibodies benefit from FcRn recycling, maintaining stable serum and tissue trough concentrations (*C*~trough~). Small nanobodies suffer from different biodistribution kinetics and potential tissue clearance fluctuations.
2. **Placebo Response Drift:** In VELA-2, the placebo HiSCR75 rate spiked to **21.6%**, compared to **17.0%** in Bimzelx's Phase 3 program. In VELA-1, Sonelokimab hit a statistically significant 17% delta. The pooled 13.8% delta was driven as much by placebo noise and site scoring variability as by molecular target engagement.

### The Pro-BAFF Thesis
Dr. May argues that HS is not purely a T-cell/neutrophil disease, but a complex multi-lineage immune disorder involving tertiary lymphoid structures and dense B-cell infiltration:
* **The 2017 Rituximab Case Study ([J Dermatol, PMID 29168221](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29168221/)):** A patient with idiopathic carpotarsal osteolysis and kidney transplant rejection received two 200 mg infusions of rituximab. While kidney rejection persisted, severe HS lesions experienced complete, durable remission for over three years.
* **Remibrutinib Phase 2 (Novartis):** A BTK inhibitor targeting B cells achieved a 60.6% sHiSCR50 response (25.9% delta) and a 16.5% HiSCR75 delta.
* **Ianalumab Phase 2 ([NCT03827798](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03827798)):** Novartis evaluated ianalumab (an anti-BAFF-R antibody) in HS, achieving a 51.6% HiSCR50 response (15.6% delta), directionally superior to Cosentyx (11% delta).

### The Forensic Counter-Audit: Why Did Novartis Terminate Ianalumab?
If BAFF inhibition is a potent, independent therapeutic mechanism in HS, why did **Novartis officially discontinue ianalumab in HS** following that Phase 2 study?

Novartis's official pipeline statement noted:
> *"Novartis will not advance investigation of ianalumab in HS following a Phase 2 POC study which did not meet our target criteria despite demonstrating efficacy vs placebo."*

Novartis had access to the full patient-level dataset—including drainage, tunnel depth, pain scores, and histologic biomarkers. If BAFF blockade delivered transformative clinical value, Novartis (which owns the dermatology sales infrastructure from Cosentyx) would have advanced ianalumab into Phase 3. Instead, they shelved it in HS while continuing it in Sjogren's and SLE.

Furthermore, in chronic inflammatory ulcers with epithelial sinus tracts, **B-cell infiltration is frequently a secondary epiphenomenon** resulting from chronic bacterial colonization (*Staphylococcus lugdunensis*, anaerobes) and exposed dermal antigens. Treating bystander B cells may dampen background inflammation without addressing the upstream follicular occlusion and neutrophil-mediated tissue necrosis.

## 6. The Pipeline Multipliers: Systemic Sclerosis & Polymyalgia Rheumatica

A central strength of May’s pitch is that Zura Bio is not a "one-trick pony." Even if HS underperforms, Zura holds two late-stage shots on goal:

### Indication 2: Systemic Sclerosis (SSc)
* **The Clinical Dilemma:** SSc is a devastating autoimmune disease characterized by cutaneous and visceral fibrosis (pulmonary fibrosis, renal crisis, esophageal dysmotility). There are **zero FDA-approved disease-modifying therapies** for diffuse cutaneous SSc.
* **The Proof of Concept:** Kyowa Kirin evaluated brodalumab (an IL-17RA receptor antagonist) in a 100-patient randomized, double-blind Phase 3 trial in Japan. Topline results presented at EULAR 2022 were staggering: **a -21.2 point mRSS delta at Week 24** (drug -16.8 vs. placebo +4.4, $p < 0.001$).

#### The Forensic Audit of Kyowa Kirin's Withdrawal
Why did Kyowa Kirin withdraw its Japanese regulatory submission in 2024 if the drug produced a -21.2 point mRSS reduction?
Our audit indicates the withdrawal was driven by **trial design artifacts and regulatory standards**, not fraudulent data:
1. The study allowed 37 of 47 placebo patients to cross over to open-label brodalumab at Week 24. By Week 52, only 7 blinded placebo controls remained. The Japanese Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (PMDA) rejected 24-week skin softening as sufficient proof of chronic disease modification without a 52-week blinded comparator.
2. Kyowa Kirin bypassed Phase 2 dose-ranging, moving directly from an $n=6$ Phase 1 into Phase 3. 

For Zura, the implications are two-fold: **IL-17 is almost certainly biologically active in SSc**, but Zura’s Phase 2 **TibuSURE (NCT06843239, n=80, reading out in May 2027)** must maintain rigid 52-week blinded control to satisfy FDA and EMA requirements.

### Indication 3: Polymyalgia Rheumatica (PMR)
* **The Medical Need:** PMR affects over 700,000 Americans aged ≥ 50. The standard of care has remained unchanged for 60 years: a 1-to-2 year oral prednisone taper. Chronic steroid exposure produces catastrophic toxicity: **30% develop diabetes, 49% develop osteoporosis, and 60–80% experience severe weight gain**.
* **Clinical Proof of Concept:**
  * **Secukinumab Phase 3 ([NEJM 2026, PMID 42234540](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42234540/)):** Cosentyx met its primary endpoint in PMR, achieving a 41.2% sustained remission rate vs. 20.4% for placebo (20.8% delta) and reducing steroid burden.
  * **Sarilumab Phase 3 ([NEJM 2023, PMID 37792612](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37792612/)):** Kevzara (IL-6R) achieved 28.3% sustained remission vs 10.3% placebo (18.0% delta) with a -1,267 mg steroid reduction, leading to FDA approval.
  * **Rituximab RCT ([Lancet Rheumatol 2023, PMID 38251523](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38251523/)):** A single infusion of rituximab achieved 52% steroid-free remission at one year vs. 21% for placebo.

#### The Forensic Audit of the PMR Market
May projects PMR as a stealth **$10B market**, extrapolating Kevzara's 42% QoQ revenue jump in Q2 2026 ($145M → $206M) into an $861M annual run rate.
However:
* Sanofi and Regeneron do not report PMR sales separately from Rheumatoid Arthritis. Q2 pharmaceutical spikes frequently reflect international wholesaler restocking, seasonal re-authorizations, and Medicaid pricing adjustments.
* Rheumatologists face deep behavioral inertia: generic prednisone costs **$15 per month**, whereas biologic therapies cost **$50,000 to $70,000 per year**. Payers enforce aggressive step-therapy, restricting biologics strictly to steroid-refractory or intolerant patients (~50% of the market). Expanding PMR into an unconstrained first-line biologic TAM will take a decade of payer education.

### The Forensic Verdict: What We Actually Think
While Dr. May has constructed an intellectually brilliant and mathematically sophisticated bull case, our forensic audit forces us to part ways with his central conclusion. Here is our probability-weighted verdict based on the biophysical, pharmacokinetic, and regulatory evidence:

1. **Probability Distribution of Readout Scenarios:**
   * **Base Case: "The Avalo / Me-Too Compression Tier" (~55% Probability):**  
     Tibulizumab's high-dose (300 mg Q2W) arm demonstrates biological activity, but achieves a HiSCR75 delta between **14% and 18%**, while the 150 mg Q2W arm shows weak or non-significant separation due to sub-therapeutic tissue concentrations. Dermatologists will view it as clinically inferior to Bimzelx (>22% delta with complete IL-17FF suppression).
   * **Downside Case: "Placebo Washout or Safety Impairment" (~30% Probability):**

     The 300 mg dose successfully matches Bimzelx (≥22% delta) while B-cell modulation provides unprecedented fistula healing without safety penalties, propelling shares to **$10.00–$12.00**.

2. **The Stance on the Evidence:**
   * **The "IL-17F is a Red Herring" argument is scientifically flawed:** In chronic HS lesions, IL-17FF is present at 30× to 100× higher molar concentrations than IL-17AA. By leaving FF completely uninhibited, tibulizumab cannot achieve the complete epithelial sterilization delivered by Bimzelx.
   * **BAFF synergy in HS lacks clinical proof:** Novartis' quiet abandonment of ianalumab in HS following Phase 2 testing is a giant red flag that B-cell targeting does not translate into primary clinical efficacy in HS.
   * **The Dilution Ceiling:** Investors must understand that even in a successful readout, Zura's $175M cash reserves require an immediate **$150M–$200M equity offering (25%–35% share dilution)** to fund Phase 3 trials across HS and SSc, creating a structural ceiling on equity gains.

3. **Bottom-Line Investment Stance:**
   * Entering the mid-November 2026 readout unhedged is an **unfavorably skewed binary gamble**, not a disciplined value investment. With a 55% probability of a lukewarm me-too outcome and a 30% probability of an outright clinical failure, the net risk/reward does not justify holding naked long common equity into the catalyst.
